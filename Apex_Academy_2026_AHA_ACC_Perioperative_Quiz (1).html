Stepwise Approach to Perioperative Cardiac Assessment

For patients with CV risk factors*, disease, or symptoms scheduled for Noncardiac Surgery (NCS).

Step 1 • Patient & Urgency Triage Entry Point
1A. CV Risk Factors, Disease, or Symptoms?

NO → Proceed directly to surgery.

YES → Check if Emergency Surgery.

1B. Emergency Surgery?

YES → Proceed to surgery.

NO → Screen for Acute Cardiac Conditions.

Step 2 • Active / Unstable Cardiac Conditions

Does the patient have: Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS), Unstable Cardiac Arrhythmias, or Decompensated Heart Failure?

YES: Management of acute cardiac condition & multidisciplinary team discussion for deferral of surgery, noninvasive treatment, or palliation.
NO: Proceed to Step 3 (Estimation of Perioperative Risk & Risk Modifiers).
Step 3 • Risk Calculator (COR 2a) & Risk Modifiers#
7 Guideline Risk Modifiers (#Conditions posing additional MACE risk):
• Severe valvular heart disease (Sec 6.4) • Severe pulmonary hypertension (Sec 6.3.2) • Elevated-risk congenital heart disease (Sec 6.3.3) • Prior coronary stents / CABG (Sec 6.1.1 & 7.5) • Recent stroke (Sec 6.7) • CIED (Pacemaker / ICD) present (Sec 6.6) • Frailty (Sec 3.3)
Low Calculated Risk (<1%) & NO Modifiers

→ Proceed to surgery.

Risk Modifier Present (Any Risk)

→ Team-based consultation regarding timing of NCS, diagnostic testing & management. Then evaluate ECG/Echo/Functional Capacity.

Elevated Calculated Risk (≥1% or RCRI >1)

→ Consider 12-lead ECG: COR 2a in established CVD/symptoms; COR 2b in asymptomatic without CVD. Consider Echo (COR 1/2a) if suspected mod-severe valve disease, unexplained dyspnea, or new/worsening LV dysfunction.

Step 4 & 5 • Functional Capacity & Preoperative Biomarker Risk Assessment
Poor or Unknown Functional Capacity (DASI ≤34, METs <4) (COR 2a)?
  • • NO (Good capacity ≥4 METs / DASI >34): Proceed to surgery (Consider GDMT initiation for long-term CV risk reduction).
  • • YES (Poor/Unknown): Ask: "Will further testing impact decision-making / perioperative care?" If NO → Proceed to surgery or consider deferral/noninvasive/palliation. If YES → Proceed to Preoperative Biomarker Risk Assessment.
Preoperative Biomarker Gatekeeper Troponin (COR 2a) or BNP/NT-proBNP (COR 2a)

Abnormal thresholds (§): Troponin >99th percentile URL; BNP >92 ng/L; NT-proBNP ≥300 ng/L.

Normal Biomarker(s)

→ Proceed to surgery.

Abnormal Biomarker(s)

→ Multidisciplinary team discussion on risks/benefits of additional cardiac evaluation (Echo, Noninvasive stress testing [COR 2b], or CCTA [COR 2b]).

Post-Evaluation: Low-risk findings → Proceed to surgery + Consider postoperative troponin surveillance (COR 2a). Elevated-risk findings (e.g., Left Main or multivessel CAD) → Consider deferral, noninvasive/invasive treatment, palliation, or proceed with postop troponin surveillance.
*CV Risk Factors: Hypertension, smoking, high cholesterol, diabetes, women age ≥65 y, men age ≥55 y, obesity, family history of premature CAD.

Management of Patients With Severe Aortic Stenosis Requiring Elective or Time-Sensitive NCS

*Severe AS definition: Aortic valve area <1.0 cm², mean gradient ≥40 mm Hg, or peak velocity Vmax ≥4.0 m/s.

Mandatory First Step
Multidisciplinary Team Discussions: Cardiology, Anesthesiology, and Surgery
Branch A

Symptomatic Severe Aortic Stenosis†

†Exertional dyspnea, angina, HF, syncope, or presyncope

CLASS 1 RECOMMENDATION
Evaluate for AVR before elective elevated-risk NCS.
Candidate for AVR?

YES → Team-based, patient-centered decision for procedural modality: SAVR vs TAVI.

NO → Consider nonsurgical management, minimally invasive alternatives, Balloon Aortic Valvuloplasty (BAV) before NCS, palliative care, or proceed with elevated-risk surgery after shared decision-making.

Branch B

Asymptomatic Severe Aortic Stenosis

Stratify by LVEF (<50% vs ≥50%) and Surgical Risk

1. LVEF < 50%

• Can delay NCS? YES → Evaluate for AVR before NCS (SAVR or TAVI).

• Can delay NCS? NO → Team-based, patient-centered decision regarding how to proceed (BAV, minimally invasive, etc.).

2. LVEF ≥ 50%

• Elevated-Risk Surgery#? NO → COR 2a Reasonable to proceed with elective low-risk NCS.

• Elevated-Risk Surgery#? YES → Team-based, patient-centered decision regarding how to proceed (or evaluate for AVR if NCS can be delayed).

#Elevated-risk surgery: Includes elevated risk for hemodynamic instability, large volume shifts, or major bleeding.

Evaluation of an Abnormal Troponin Obtained for Postoperative Surveillance

Triggered when postoperative Troponin >99th percentile URL with an acute rise/fall pattern.

Initial Action
Postoperative Myocardial Injury (Troponin >99th URL*) → Obtain 12-Lead ECG + History & Physical
Pathway 1 • ST-Segment Elevations Present? YES

Postoperative STEMI (COR 1)

Patients who develop STEMI after NCS should receive GDMT including consideration of invasive coronary angiography, balancing bleeding and thrombotic risks with severity of clinical presentation.

Pathway 2 • ST-Segment Elevations? NO

Is Injury Likely Due to a Nonischemic Process?

YES (e.g., PE, Sepsis, Acute Decompensated HF, Acute Stroke): Classified as Nonischemic Myocardial Injury → Treat underlying cause.

NO (Ischemic Etiology Suspected): Evaluate against Universal Definition of MI criteria below.

Has at least 1 criterion been met? (1) Symptoms of acute myocardial ischemia, (2) New ischemic ECG changes, (3) Pathologic Q waves, (4) Imaging evidence of new loss of viable myocardium or new RWMA, (5) Coronary thrombus on angiography.

@Apex High-Yield Summary

2026 AHA/ACC Perioperative Rapid-Fire Pearls

Key clinical takeaways for board exams and inpatient consults.

💊

Beta-Blockers & Statins

  • Chronic Beta-Blockers: Continue perioperatively (Class 1).
  • De Novo Beta-Blockers: NEVER initiate on the day of surgery (Class 3: Harm — increases stroke and mortality).
  • Statins: Continue in all patients currently taking them; initiate preoperatively in patients with clinical ASCVD or vascular surgery indications.
🧪

Biomarker Cutoffs & MINS

  • Preop Biomarker Thresholds: BNP >92 ng/L, NT-proBNP ≥300 ng/L, or Troponin >99th percentile URL.
  • DASI Score: A Duke Activity Status Index (DASI) score ≤34 (or <4 METs) defines poor functional capacity in the 2026 algorithm.
  • MINS: Elevated postop troponin without overt ischemic symptoms/ECG changes; warrants outpatient cardiology follow-up (COR 2a).
🩸

DOACs, Warfarin & SGLT2i

  • Dabigatran + CrCl <50 mL/min: Hold for 4 days prior to high-bleeding-risk surgery (vs 2 days if CrCl ≥50).
  • Warfarin Bridging: Reserved ONLY for high thrombotic risk (mechanical mitral valve, CHA2DS2-VASc ≥7, stroke/VTE <3 mo).
  • SGLT2 Inhibitors: Hold 3 days before surgery (4 days for ertugliflozin) to prevent euglycemic DKA.
🫀

Stents & Severe Aortic Stenosis

  • Prior PCI: Delay elective NCS ≥12 months after ACS PCI, ≥6 months after stable CAD DES (time-sensitive NCS may proceed after ≥3 months if DAPT impact weighed).
  • Asymptomatic Severe AS: If LVEF ≥50%, it is reasonable (COR 2a) to proceed with elective low-risk NCS. If LVEF <50% and surgery can be delayed, evaluate for AVR first.
